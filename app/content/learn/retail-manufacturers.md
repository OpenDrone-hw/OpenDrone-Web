## Retailers

### HobbyKing

- HobbyKing is not one company: the FCC identified it as "the trade name of several affiliated companies", specifically ABC Fulfillment Services LLC (doing business as HobbyKing USA LLC and HobbyKing.com) and Indubitably, Inc. (doing business as HobbyKing Corp., HobbyKing USA LLC, HobbyKing, and HobbyKing.com), plus a related Hong Kong company, Hextronik LTD. Source: FCC Forfeiture Order FCC 20-101, File No. EB-SED-17-00023762, https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-20-101A1.pdf [verified] (primary: FCC adjudication)
- The Hong Kong entity behind HobbyKing is Hextronik LTD, described in HobbyKing's own Letter of Inquiry response as "part [of] HobbyKing's marketing setup". Source: FCC 20-101 paragraph 30 and footnote 84, https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-20-101A1.pdf [verified] (primary)
- Anthony Hand is named by the FCC as "Owner and Director" of both ABC Fulfillment Services LLC and Indubitably, Inc., with service made through registered agents Davies Pearson P.C. (Tacoma, WA) and Corporation Service Company (Wilmington, DE). Source: FCC 20-101 paragraph 45, https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-20-101A1.pdf [verified] (primary). This is the only official confirmation of HobbyKing ownership located.
- The commonly repeated founding story, 1 April 2001 in Hong Kong by Karen Wu and Anthony Hand, is uncited in its usual source: the Wikipedia infobox carries no citation and the History paragraph cites only a BigSquidRC category page and a Flite Test interview. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HobbyKing and its raw wikitext at https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=HobbyKing&action=raw [verified as to the absence of a citation] (direct inspection). Treat the 2001 date as unsourced.
- The Internet Archive contradicts a 2001 founding for the RC retail business: hobbyking.com captures from 2000 and 2005 are a parked "Unavailable Site" page and a link farm, not a store. Sources: https://web.archive.org/web/20001010051010/http://www.hobbyking.com/ and https://web.archive.org/web/20050122044118/http://hobbyking.com/ [verified] (primary: archival captures). The early hobbyking.com HTTP 200 captures are therefore parked pages, not evidence of trading.
- The actual predecessor store was HobbyCity, trading as United Hobbies: hobbycity.com was a news-scraper page on 6 February 2006 and a full "hobbycity :: OnLine Store" RC shop by 26 October 2007, whose index redirects to UNITEDHOBBIES/store/uh_index.asp. The RC store therefore started between February 2006 and October 2007. Sources: https://web.archive.org/web/20060206132006/http://www.hobbycity.com/ and https://web.archive.org/web/20071026101621/http://www.hobbycity.com/ [verified] (two primary archival captures)
- The United Hobbies lineage persisted in HobbyKing's own URLs: its About Us page lived at hobbyking.com/hobbyking/store/uh_aboutUs.asp, keeping the uh_ prefix. Source: Wikipedia raw wikitext citation, https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=HobbyKing&action=raw [verified] (corroborating artefact)
- As late as June 2009 the hobbyking.com homepage still served "Hobbycity Online R/C Hobby Store", so the HobbyKing brand name is later than the business. Source: https://web.archive.org/web/20090601000000/http://www.hobbyking.com/ [verified] (primary archival)
- Turnigy predates the HobbyKing name: the October 2007 HobbyCity category tree already lists hexTronik, HXT Outrunners, Turnigy Motors, TowerPro and KDA Outrunners. Source: https://web.archive.org/web/20071026101621/http://www.hobbycity.com/ [verified] (primary archival)
- The house brands have since contracted sharply: a live site search on 12 August 2026 returns a Turnigy brand facet with 181 products, a MultiStar facet with 1 product, and zero results for "afro-esc". Sources: https://hobbyking.com/en_us/catalogsearch/result/?q=turnigy , https://hobbyking.com/en_us/catalogsearch/result/?q=multistar , https://hobbyking.com/en_us/catalogsearch/result/?q=afro-esc [verified] (primary: live site, dated observation)
- HobbyKing is still trading as of 12 August 2026, with 2026-dated blog posts. Source: https://hobbyking.com/en_us/blog/ [verified] (primary: live site)
- HobbyKing sold "dozens of models of audio/video transmitters (AV transmitters) for use with unmanned aircraft systems" to US consumers without checking whether they were FCC compliant, over a period of years. Source: FCC 20-101 paragraph 2, https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-20-101A1.pdf [verified] (primary)
- Several HobbyKing AV transmitter models "were capable of operating in spectrum bands used by, and causing interference to, important Federal government systems" and some "operated at power levels above those permitted". Source: FCC 20-101 paragraph 2, https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-20-101A1.pdf [verified] (primary). This is the regulatory core of why cheap 1.2 GHz and out-of-band VTX sales became untenable in the US.
- The FCC's Spectrum Enforcement Division first investigated HobbyKing in 2016 and issued a Marketing Citation, then opened a second investigation in 2017 after further complaints. Source: FCC 20-101 paragraphs 8 and 9, https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-20-101A1.pdf [verified] (primary)
- HobbyKing's defence in the second investigation was that it "was not marketing AV transmitters in the United States", which the FCC rejected. Source: FCC 20-101 paragraph 9, https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-20-101A1.pdf [verified] (primary)
- HobbyKing had a New York office and US customer service operations at the time of the FCC action, per HobbyKing's own careers website as cited by the FCC. Source: FCC 20-101 paragraph 7 and footnote 8, https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-20-101A1.pdf [verified] (primary)
- HobbyKing claimed inability to pay but supplied three years of tax returns for only one of the two named entities, Indubitably Inc., and none for ABC Fulfillment Services LLC or Hextronik LTD, so the FCC refused any reduction. Source: FCC 20-101 paragraphs 29 to 31, https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-20-101A1.pdf [verified] (primary). This is the closest thing to a public financial disclosure from HobbyKing and it shows the group deliberately did not disclose group finances.
- HobbyKing stated that ABC Fulfillment Services LLC "was involved in fulfillment services only until the end of 2015", which the FCC called too vague to accept. Source: FCC 20-101 paragraph 30, https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-20-101A1.pdf [verified] (primary). This is a dated hint at an internal restructuring of the US fulfilment arm.
- HobbyKing at peak ran seven regional warehouses across Hong Kong, Australia, the United States, the Netherlands, the United Kingdom and China, and was down to four (Europe, Hong Kong, UK, US) as of 2024. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HobbyKing [single] (needs Wayback confirmation of the warehouse selector page across years to promote to verified)
- Toby Osmond was named HobbyKing CEO in January 2019. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HobbyKing citing https://www.linkedin.com/posts/hobbyking_we-are-proud-to-announce-our-new-ceo-as-toby-activity-6448065065588523008-EaNe [single]
- HobbyKing's house brands include Turnigy, Multistar, Afro, ZIPPY, OrangeRX and the KK flight controller line, and the FCC order names "OrangeRX DSMX DSM2 Compatible 2.4Ghz" as one of the specific noncompliant products it kept marketing. Source: FCC 20-101 line 617, https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-20-101A1.pdf [verified for OrangeRX as a HobbyKing-marketed brand] (the full house-brand list is [single], from https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HobbyKing)

### GetFPV and Lumenier

- GetFPV was founded in 2011 and "began... as a small garage-based operation in Sarasota, Florida". Source: https://www.getfpv.com/about [verified] (primary: company about page, corroborated by the 7 January 2012 first Internet Archive capture of getfpv.com)
- Lumenier and GetFPV are two segments of one company, not two firms: Lumenier is the manufacturer and GetFPV.com is the online store that sells Lumenier products alongside other brands. Sources: https://www.inc.com/will-yakowicz/lumenier-sells-majority-stake-to-pfingsten.html and https://pfingsten.com/portfolio/lumenier-2/ [verified] (two independent sources, one a private equity owner's own portfolio page). This resolves the house-brand question: Lumenier is GetFPV's in-house brand, under shared ownership.
- lumenier.com's first Internet Archive capture (15 May 2010) predates getfpv.com's (7 January 2012), so the brand existed before the storefront rather than being spun out of it. Source: Wayback CDX API per domain [verified] (primary: capture records)
- Tim Nilson founded both GetFPV and Lumenier. His prior career was in digital music: N2K in New York, co-founder of Run Media and Run Tones (both acquired by Sony Music Entertainment in 2002), then a decade at Sony Music. Sources: https://nilsonholdings.com/tim-nilson and https://www.inc.com/will-yakowicz/lumenier-sells-majority-stake-to-pfingsten.html [verified] (two independent sources)
- On 29 September 2017 Lumenier closed a leveraged buyout selling a majority stake to the Chicago private equity firm Pfingsten Partners, with Nilson retaining equity and staying on as CEO. Terms were not disclosed. Sources: https://www.inc.com/will-yakowicz/lumenier-sells-majority-stake-to-pfingsten.html , https://pfingsten.com/portfolio/lumenier-2/ and https://eu.heraldtribune.com/story/special/2017/10/05/private-equity-firm-buys-sarasotas-lumenier-and-getfpv/18571184007/ [verified] (three sources including the acquirer's own page). The largest US FPV retailer has been private-equity controlled since 2017, which is not widely understood in the community.
- The Florida registry corroborates the private equity structure: LUMENIER HOLDCO LLC (document M17000008366, a Delaware LLC registered in Florida on 29 September 2017) lists managers Phillip D Bronsteatter and David H Johnson at 300 N LaSalle St, Suite 5400, Chicago IL, which is the sponsor's office. Source: https://search.sunbiz.org/Inquiry/CorporationSearch/SearchResults?inquiryType=EntityName&searchNameOrder=LUMENIER&searchTerm=LUMENIER [verified] (primary: state business registry)
- GETFPV LLC is a Delaware LLC registered in Florida on 27 February 2017, document M17000001666, status active, principal address 1060 Goodrich Ave, Sarasota FL 34236. Source: https://search.sunbiz.org/Inquiry/CorporationSearch/SearchResults?inquiryType=EntityName&searchNameOrder=GETFPV&searchTerm=GETFPV [verified] (primary: state business registry)
- Pfingsten's portfolio page states Lumenier "is headquartered in Sarasota, Florida and has an additional facility in Shenzhen, China", so the largest US FPV retailer runs its own China operation rather than buying purely at arm's length. Source: https://pfingsten.com/portfolio/lumenier-2/ [verified] (primary: owner's disclosure)
- GetFPV is named as the benchmark competitor by the only publicly traded FPV retailer: "The primary retail competitor is GetFPV." Source: Unusual Machines 10-K FY2024, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1956955/000168316825001929/unusual_i10k-123124.htm [verified] (primary: SEC filing). This is the closest thing to independent confirmation that GetFPV leads the US market.

### Marketplaces and the structural forces on cross-border FPV retail

- banggood.com first returns HTTP 200 in the Internet Archive on 8 December 2008, and aliexpress.com on 1 August 2009, so both channels predate the FPV racing boom by roughly six years and were general marketplaces that FPV grew into, not FPV-specific ventures. Source: http://web.archive.org/cdx/search/cdx?url=banggood.com&output=json&limit=1&filter=statuscode:200&from=1996 and the same query for aliexpress.com [verified] (primary: Internet Archive capture records)
- The EU abolished the VAT exemption for low value imported consignments, the roughly 22 euro relief, by deleting Title IV of Directive 2009/132/EC via Council Directive (EU) 2017/2455 of 5 December 2017. Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2017/2455/oj [verified] (primary: EU legislation)
- That abolition, originally set for 1 January 2021, was deferred to 1 July 2021 by Council Decision (EU) 2020/1109 of 20 July 2020 because of COVID-19. Sources: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec/2020/1109/oj and https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2017/2455/oj [verified] (two primary EU legal sources). 1 July 2021 is therefore the correct date for when small direct-from-China FPV parcels stopped arriving into the EU VAT free, and any account citing 1 January 2021 is quoting the superseded date.
- The replacement regime charges import VAT on consignments under 150 euro through a special scheme, the Import One Stop Shop. Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2017/2455/oj [verified] (primary)
- EU drone operating rules come from Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2019/947 of 24 May 2019, published 11 June 2019 in OJ L 152, applicable from 1 July 2020. Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2019/947/oj [verified] (primary)
- Regulation (EU) 2019/947 requires the remote pilot to "keep the unmanned aircraft in VLOS at all times" in the open category, with VLOS defined as "continuous unaided visual contact", which is the rule that puts long range FPV outside the open category in Europe. Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2019/947/oj [verified] (primary)
- The open category splits into A1, A2 and A3 subcategories distinguished by proximity to uninvolved people, with A2 requiring remote pilot certification. Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2019/947/oj [verified] (primary)
- expresslrs.org first returns HTTP 200 in the Internet Archive on 26 July 2021, which bounds when the ExpressLRS project had a public project site, though the code predates the site. Source: http://web.archive.org/cdx/search/cdx?url=expresslrs.org&output=json&limit=1&filter=statuscode:200&from=1996 [verified] (primary: capture record)
- runcam.com first returns HTTP 200 on 21 April 2013, foxeer.com on 9 January 2016, and caddxfpv.com on 21 June 2019, giving a rough order of arrival for the three main FPV camera brands. Source: Wayback CDX API per domain [verified] (primary: capture records)

### RaceDayQuads and Tyler Brennan's regulatory activism

- The petitioners in the Remote ID challenge were "TYLER BRENNAN AND RACEDAYQUADS LLC", so the shop itself was a named party, not just its owner. Source: Brennan v. Dickson, No. 21-1087, D.C. Cir., official opinion PDF, https://api.govinfo.gov/packages/USCOURTS-caDC-21-01087/granules/USCOURTS-caDC-21-01087-0/pdf [verified] (primary: court opinion)
- The case was argued 15 December 2021 and decided 29 July 2022, before Circuit Judges Pillard, Wilkins and Walker, with the opinion written by Judge Pillard. Source: Brennan v. Dickson, 45 F.4th 48, https://api.govinfo.gov/packages/USCOURTS-caDC-21-01087/granules/USCOURTS-caDC-21-01087-0/pdf [verified] (primary)
- Brennan lost outright: "For all these reasons, we deny the petition for review." Source: Brennan v. Dickson conclusion, https://api.govinfo.gov/packages/USCOURTS-caDC-21-01087/granules/USCOURTS-caDC-21-01087-0/pdf [verified] (primary)
- Brennan's legal theory was that Remote ID is warrantless government surveillance under the Fourth Amendment, plus four Administrative Procedure Act arguments: improper reliance on ex parte communications, provisions not a logical outgrowth of the proposed rule, failure to consult statutorily specified entities, and failure to address material comments. Source: Brennan v. Dickson pages 3 to 5 and 15, https://api.govinfo.gov/packages/USCOURTS-caDC-21-01087/granules/USCOURTS-caDC-21-01087-0/pdf [verified] (primary)
- The court rejected the Fourth Amendment claim on the ground that "It is hard to see what could be private about flying a drone in the open air", holding that a facial challenge fails because drones are virtually always flown in public. Source: Brennan v. Dickson page 16, https://api.govinfo.gov/packages/USCOURTS-caDC-21-01087/granules/USCOURTS-caDC-21-01087-0/pdf [verified] (primary)
- Petitioners were represented by Jonathan Rupprecht, with Elizabeth Candelario and Kathleen Yodice on the briefs, and the Association for Unmanned Vehicle Systems International filed an amicus brief supporting the FAA, so the established commercial drone industry lobby was on the opposite side from the FPV retailer. Source: Brennan v. Dickson caption pages, https://api.govinfo.gov/packages/USCOURTS-caDC-21-01087/granules/USCOURTS-caDC-21-01087-0/pdf [verified] (primary)
- Tyler Brennan is described in the opinion as "an Air Force pilot and self-proclaimed 'avid drone user'". Source: Brennan v. Dickson page 15, https://api.govinfo.gov/packages/USCOURTS-caDC-21-01087/granules/USCOURTS-caDC-21-01087-0/pdf [verified] (primary, quoting Brennan's own brief)
- Brennan told the court that RaceDayQuads "has served more than 40,000 different customers per year in its almost four years of existence", a rare disclosed scale figure for an FPV shop, measured as unique customers per year and self-reported in a legal brief around 2021. Source: Brennan v. Dickson page 15, https://api.govinfo.gov/packages/USCOURTS-caDC-21-01087/granules/USCOURTS-caDC-21-01087-0/pdf [verified] (primary, but note it is a party's own unaudited assertion)
- The "almost four years of existence" claim is consistent with the Internet Archive record: racedayquads.com first returns HTTP 200 on 1 January 2017. Source: http://web.archive.org/cdx/search/cdx?url=racedayquads.com&output=json&limit=1&filter=statuscode:200&from=1996 [verified] (primary: capture record, independently corroborating the brief)
- The FAA received approximately 53,000 public comments on the Remote ID NPRM, the figure both the final rule and the D.C. Circuit use. Sources: Remote Identification of Unmanned Aircraft, 86 FR 4390, https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2021/01/15/2020-28948/remote-identification-of-unmanned-aircraft and Brennan v. Dickson page 4 [verified] (two primary sources). The community comment campaign that RaceDayQuads promoted is the visible cause of that volume, though the rule text does not name RaceDayQuads.
- Neither the Remote ID final rule text nor the D.C. Circuit opinion attributes the comment volume to RaceDayQuads by name: a text search of the full 86 FR 4390 rule returns zero occurrences of "RaceDayQuads". Source: full text of 86 FR 4390, https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2021/01/15/2020-28948.txt [verified] (direct search of primary text). Any claim that RDQ "drove 53,000 comments" is a community attribution, not a documented one.
- The Remote ID rulemaking timeline is: NPRM published 31 December 2019 at 84 FR 72438, final rule published 15 January 2021 at 86 FR 4390, effective 16 March 2021, with operator compliance required from 16 September 2023. Sources: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2019/12/31/2019-28100/remote-identification-of-unmanned-aircraft-systems and https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2021/01/15/2020-28948/remote-identification-of-unmanned-aircraft [verified] (primary: Federal Register)

### Team BlackSheep, the vertically integrated outlier

- TBS names three legal entities on its own site: a Singapore entity with UEN 202504062K, the Hong Kong company Blacksheep Avionics Co Ltd (BR number 59435080), and the Austrian company Conceptos Pirker GmbH (UID ATU62770139). Source: https://www.team-blacksheep.com/about [verified] (primary: company's own disclosure with registration numbers)
- TBS splits functions by geography: Singapore for headquarters, R&D and support, Hong Kong for factory and logistics, and an Austrian/European office in St. Anton am Arlberg. Source: https://www.team-blacksheep.com/about [verified] (primary). Owning the factory step is what makes TBS the vertically integrated outlier: most western FPV brands own only design and marketing.
- The Singapore UEN 202504062K encodes a 2025 registration year under Singapore's UEN format, so the Singapore headquarters entity is recent and the group has restructured. Source: https://www.team-blacksheep.com/about read against the ACRA UEN format [single] (format inference, not confirmed against an ACRA record)
- TBS gives its founding year as 2007 and says the name came from being "the first FPV pilots in Switzerland and Austria to fly with goggles". Source: https://www.team-blacksheep.com/about [single] (company self-description)
- TBS's founder is Raphael Pirker, known as "Trappy". Source: https://www.team-blacksheep.com/about [verified] (primary: company's own about page)
- TBS's own account of the FAA case is that "In 2011, the FAA fined Raphael for operating an aircraft while flying FPV in Virginia", that the allegation was reduced, and that it produced roughly "8 months of relatively unrestricted flying" for FPV pilots. Source: https://www.team-blacksheep.com/about [single] (self-serving account, the 8 month claim is not independently confirmed here)
- In Huerta v. Pirker an administrative law judge dismissed the FAA's enforcement action in March 2014 on the basis that model aircraft are not legally "aircraft", and the NTSB reversed on 17 November 2014, holding that model aircraft are "aircraft" under 14 CFR 91.13 and remanding on whether the operation was reckless. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-person_view_(radio_control) [single] (secondary summary, the NTSB order itself was not retrieved, see Unknowns)

### Rotor Riot and the public-company window into FPV retail

- Rotor Riot, LLC was an Ohio limited liability company with three members, acquired by Red Cat Holdings, Inc. (Nevada, SEC CIK 0000748268, ticker RCAT) via an Agreement of Merger entered into on 31 December 2019 and consummated 23 January 2020. Source: Red Cat 8-K filed 2020-01-29, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/748268/000155479520000021/rcat0129form8k.htm [verified] (primary: SEC filing)
- Rotor Riot began as a Flite Test brand: Rotor Riot, LLC was incorporated in Ohio in December 2016 and in the same month bought the Rotor Riot trademark, video content and products from Flite Test, LLC for 150,000 USD, then licensed them straight back to Flite Test at a 10 percent of net sales royalty. Source: audited financial statements of Rotor Riot, LLC, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/748268/000155479520000092/rcat0406form8kaexh99_1.htm [verified] (primary: audited financial statements). This is the documented Flite Test connection.
- Rotor Riot was a pure licensor until March 2019, when it terminated the Flite Test licence and began selling products itself through rotorriot.com. Source: same audited statements [verified] (primary). This is why 2018 revenue was royalties only and 2019 revenue jumped more than twelvefold.
- Chad Kapper is identified in Red Cat's SEC filings as Rotor Riot's founder and Chief Executive Officer, and as managing member of Brains Riding in Tanks, LLC (BRIT), Rotor Riot's majority owner. Sources: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/748268/000155479521000154/rcat0430prospectus424b4.htm and https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/748268/000155479520000021/rcat0129form8k.htm [verified] (primary: SEC filings)
- Red Cat's headline purchase price for Rotor Riot was 3,700,000 USD, less approximately 915,563 USD of Rotor Riot, BRIT and Kapper debt and payables, divided by a 20-day VWAP of 1.25445 USD, producing 2,219,650 Red Cat shares to Rotor Riot's members. Source: Red Cat 8-K, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/748268/000155479520000021/rcat0129form8k.htm [verified] (primary)
- The Rotor Riot acquisition consideration was Red Cat common stock: the members' 100 percent of Rotor Riot's membership interests "would be converted into shares of common stock of the Company". Source: Red Cat 8-K filed 2020-01-29, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/748268/000155479520000021/rcat0129form8k.htm [verified] (primary). Note this was a stock deal, not cash, which is what a small drone retailer was worth to a microcap in 2020.
- The Rotor Riot merger closing slipped twice: the original termination date of 14 January 2020 was extended to 17 January 2020 by a first amendment, then to 24 January 2020 by a second amendment which also swapped the Ohio acquisition subsidiary for a Delaware one. Source: Red Cat 8-K filed 2020-01-29, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/748268/000155479520000021/rcat0129form8k.htm [verified] (primary)
- Rotor Riot LLC's audited revenue was 1,897,352 USD for the year ended 31 December 2019, against 150,410 USD for 2018, with a 2019 gross margin of 509,061 USD (26.8 percent) and a net loss of 89,650 USD. ## ODM reality

- Unusual Machines, which owns Fat Shark and Rotor Riot, states in its 10-K that "Our contract manufacturers generally purchase these components on our behalf from approved suppliers", so a well-known FPV goggle brand does not manufacture its own goggles. Source: UMAC 10-K for FY2024, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1956955/000168316825001929/unusual_i10k-123124.htm [verified] (primary: SEC filing)
- The same 10-K states "Fat Shark and Rotor Riot rely on third party suppliers for the resources needed" and calls out the contrast with being "more vertically integrated", which is an explicit admission that the brand-plus-outsourced-manufacturing model is the norm in FPV. Source: UMAC 10-K FY2024, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1956955/000168316825001929/unusual_i10k-123124.htm [verified] (primary)
- Unusual Machines states it "purchases inventory from approximately 50 suppliers" as of the FY2024 10-K, which gives a sense of how fragmented FPV component sourcing is even for a single brand portfolio. Source: UMAC 10-K FY2024, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1956955/000168316825001929/unusual_i10k-123124.htm [verified] (primary)
- Happymodel advertises "OEM & ODM, Overall solution" including "appearance and structure design" and "electronic research and development" on its own corporate site, which is direct first-party evidence that a recognised FPV brand also manufactures under other companies' labels. Source: https://www.happymodel.cn/index.php/about-us/ [verified] (primary)
- Fat Shark "currently has no equipment redundancy to manufacture its products", per the FY2024 10-K risk factors, and Rotor Riot "is limited by the number of personnel it has on staff to assemble drones and drone parts", so even the branded assembly work is small-scale and manual. Source: UMAC 10-K FY2024, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1956955/000168316825001929/unusual_i10k-123124.htm [verified] (primary)

## Business models

- US tariffs are a live cost input on FPV retail: Unusual Machines states "The United States has continuously increased tariffs since 2019, which Unusual Machines is currently subject to and range from 2% to 25%", plus "in Q1 2025, additional tariffs have been instituted on Chinese products". Source: UMAC 10-K FY2024, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1956955/000168316825001929/unusual_i10k-123124.htm [verified] (primary)
- The company states plainly that "These tariffs increase the cost of goods which reduces the company's profit margins or increases the total price to our customers", which is the mechanism by which US FPV retail prices decoupled from AliExpress prices. Source: UMAC 10-K FY2024, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1956955/000168316825001929/unusual_i10k-123124.htm [verified] (primary)
- FPV retail gross margin is thin: Unusual Machines reported approximately 28 percent gross margin on retail sales in Q2 2024 and 29 percent blended. Source: UMAC shareholder letter, 14 August 2024, filed as 8-K Exhibit 99.1, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1956955/000168316824005692/umac_ex9901.htm [verified] (primary)

Source: UMAC 10-K FY2025, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1956955/000168316826001730/umac_i10k-123125.htm [verified] (primary). This is the clearest documented case of the FPV supply base pivoting to defence.
- Red Cat's CEO Jeffrey Thompson sat as a director of Unusual Machines while Red Cat sold Fat Shark and Rotor Riot to it, and Red Cat held 48.66 percent of Unusual Machines' common stock at closing, so the two companies were not arm's length. Source: UMAC 8-K, 22 February 2024, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1956955/000168316824001122/umac_8k.htm [verified] (primary)

Environment limits that shaped this pass, stated so the next pass does not repeat the dead ends:

- General web search was unavailable: the session's search budget was exhausted early, and Bing, DuckDuckGo (both html and lite endpoints), Startpage, Ecosia, Mojeek and public searx instances are all bot-blocked or captcha-walled from this environment. Everything above was obtained by fetching known URLs directly or by querying open APIs.
- Working evidence routes, for reuse: Wayback CDX API, Federal Register API, SEC EDGAR full-text search and submissions JSON, CourtListener search API v4 (no auth), GovInfo API with DEMO_KEY for official court PDFs, docs.fcc.gov for FCC orders, EUR-Lex ELI URLs, Wikipedia API.
